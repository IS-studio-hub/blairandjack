Science

Razor bumps might look small, but they have a big impact on comfort, confidence, and the skin you live in every day.

Impact of shaving
irritation on men’s skin:

Pseudofolliculitis barbae — razor bumps — happens when curly or coarse hairs curve back into the skin after a shave. The result is inflammation, tenderness, and those persistent bumps men are told to just live with.

Each irritated follicle is an inflammatory event. Left untreated, redness lingers, skin feels hot or tight, and the next shave starts from an already compromised barrier.

Close shaves and coarse hair make ingrowns more likely — especially along the neck and jaw. Treating the bump at the source helps keep hairs from trapping under the skin again.

Repeated inflammation can leave darker marks, particularly on deeper skin tones. Calming the bump early is one of the most effective ways to keep the complexion looking even.

Harsh scrubs, alcohol-heavy aftershaves, and over-drying actives strip the barrier. Irritated skin cannot heal. Our formulas treat the problem without wrecking the moisture layer that keeps skin calm.

Shaving removes oil along with hair. Without hydration that won’t clog pores, skin tightens, flakes, and becomes more reactive to the next pass of the razor.

Bumps are not only a skin issue. They change how a collar feels, how a date feels, how you show up. Treating them is daily care — not vanity.

Men’s shelves are full of fragrance-heavy splashes and watered-down “for him” versions of other lines. They rarely target inflammation, ingrowns, and barrier repair together — which is why Blair & Jack exists.
